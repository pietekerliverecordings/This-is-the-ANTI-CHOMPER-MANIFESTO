The Anti-Chomper Manifesto

Because apparently this needs to be said.

A band. An audience. Some microphones.

Three things that should work together beautifully.

And then there's you, having a fucking conversation.

Welcome to the concert. The band is playing. The guitarist is doing something incredible. The crowd is locked in. A taper is capturing the whole thing for people who weren't lucky enough to be there.

And you're explaining your weekend to Dave.

Dave doesn't need to know. We all need you to shut up.

What the fuck is a chomper?

A chomper is someone who treats a concert as background music for a conversation that absolutely could have happened tomorrow.

It's the guy discussing work during the quietest song of the set. It's the couple recapping their holiday over a guitar solo. It's the drunk philosopher who has just discovered the meaning of life and needs to share it with everyone within microphone range.

It's you, if you're doing this shit.

Some of us are recording this.

Those little microphones aren't fashion accessories.

Someone spent serious money on microphones, cables, preamps and recorders. Someone spent hours getting the levels right. Someone will spend more hours editing, mastering and sharing the recording for free.

All to preserve a beautiful moment in live music.

And your contribution is a 14-minute discussion about parking.

Congratulations. You're now part of the archive.

A special message to Phish fans

The band has spent 25 minutes building a jam from a whisper into a wall of sound that makes the universe briefly make sense.

Then, during the quietest passage, you lean over and say:

"Bro, remember that time in Vegas?"

No, bro. We're trying to remember this fucking jam.

Enjoy the music. Dance. Cheer. Lose your mind.

Just don't mistake a 30-minute jam for a 30-minute opportunity to tell your buddy a story.

We're not asking for silence.

This is a concert, not a library.

We want the applause. The cheers. The laughter. The collective roar when the opening notes hit. The sound of thousands of people experiencing something together.

That's what makes a great audience recording an audience recording.

We want the crowd. We don't want your fucking podcast.

It's really not that complicated.

  • The band is playing. Listen.
  • Need to talk? Take it somewhere else.
  • See microphones? Remember that sound travels.
  • Someone asks you to keep it down? Don't be an asshole.
  • Having a great time? Excellent. Let everyone else have one too.

One last thing.

Somewhere, years from now, someone will discover a recording of a legendary show. They'll put on their headphones. They'll close their eyes. They'll hear a performance that would otherwise have been lost to time.

And then they'll hear you explaining cryptocurrency.

Don't be that person.

You came to hear the band.

Hear the fucking band.

Respect the music. Respect the recording. Don't be a chomper.

An entirely unnecessary manifesto about an entirely avoidable problem. Written by a taper who would really like to hear the guitar solo.
